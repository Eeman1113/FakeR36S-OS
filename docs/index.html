Install FakeR36S-OS

Custom firmware for fake R36S handhelds on the GA36-MB board with an Allwinner A23.

Your card image is built in this browser. Nothing is uploaded.
Best opened on a computer This builds an SD card image and writes it to a card, so you need a desktop or laptop with a card reader. Read it here by all means, then come back on a PC or Mac when you are ready to install.

    Start here

    What are you doing today?

    Step 1 of 5

    What you need

    Check you have all four before starting. The first one is the only one you cannot buy.

    • 1
      The SD card that came with the console It holds the bootloader, which cannot legally be shared. Without it nothing can boot.
    • 2
      A second, blank microSD card 8 GB or larger. Your original card stays untouched as a backup.
    • 3
      Chrome or Edge This page writes to drives, which other browsers do not allow.
    • 4
      An SD card writer Raspberry Pi Imager or balenaEtcher. Both are free, both work everywhere.
    This browser cannot write to drives. You can still build your image here, but the automatic steps will ask you to copy files by hand. Chrome or Edge makes it one click.
    Is your console actually an A23? On the stock firmware open System Information and look at CPU NUMBER. 2 means A23 and you are in the right place. 4 means A33, and you want goodluckOS instead. The next step checks this for you anyway.

    Step 2 of 5

    Back up the stock card

    Put the console's card in your computer. You are going to copy it to a file. This only reads the card, it does not change it.

    Open Terminal. First find the card, then copy it.

    diskutil list external physical

    Look for the one whose size matches your card, for example /dev/disk4. Put that number in the next command, and check it twice.

    sudo dd if=/dev/rdisk4 of=~/stock_backup.img bs=4m status=progress

    About 15 minutes for a 16 GB card. The r in rdisk is not a typo, it is much faster.

    Download Win32 Disk Imager, then:

    1. Pick the card's drive letter under Device
    2. Under Image File, type a new file name ending in stock_backup.img
    3. Click Read, not Write
    If Windows pops up asking to format the card, click Cancel. The card uses Linux partitions Windows cannot read. Formatting would destroy the bootloader.
    lsblk

    Find your card, for example /dev/sdb, and check the size matches.

    sudo dd if=/dev/sdb of=~/stock_backup.img bs=4M status=progress conv=fsync
    Get the device name right. Pointing this at the wrong disk reads the wrong thing, and a typo in the later write step can erase your computer's drive. Check the size matches your card before pressing enter.
    Can I copy less than the whole card?

    Yes. Only the first 128 MB is needed. Add count=128 on macOS with bs=1m, or count=32 on Linux with bs=4M. A full backup is still better, because it also preserves the games and saves that shipped on the card.

    I lost the original card

    The bootloader belongs to the manufacturer and cannot be shipped with this project, so it has to come from your own card. Any old image or backup of it works too.

    Lost it entirely? Other owners of the same board have been known to leave dumps floating around out at sea. wink wink

    Step 3 of 5

    Build your image

    Choose the stock_backup.img you just made. This page reads 128 KB of it to work out exactly which board you have, then builds an image for it.

    Build

    Downloads FakeR36S-OS (about 380 MB), then adds the boot region from your own card and your boot logo. The download is the slow part.

    This browser blocked the download. Grab goodluck_base_a23_panel2.img.gz from the release page yourself, then pick it here:

    Save fakers36s.img

    Step 4 of 5

    Write it to the new card

    Use the blank card, not the stock one. Your original card is the only copy of the bootloader that belongs to your console. Keep it safe.

    The easy way

    Open Raspberry Pi Imager:

    1. Choose OS, scroll to the bottom, Use custom, pick fakers36s.img
    2. Choose Storage, pick the blank card
    3. Write, and say no if it offers to apply customisation settings

    balenaEtcher works the same way: image, drive, flash.

    Command line instead 
    sudo dd if=fakers36s.img of=/dev/rdisk4 bs=4m status=progress   # macOS
sudo dd if=fakers36s.img of=/dev/sdb    bs=4M status=progress   # Linux

    This one writes, so a wrong device name destroys whatever is on it. Check twice.

    Step 5 of 5

    First boot

    Put the new card in slot TF1, the one the stock card came out of, and power on.

    You will see the logo, a chime, then EmulationStation. The first boot takes longer than usual because it grows the games partition to fill your card.

    Black screen but the device seems alive? You may have the other panel.

    Add your games

    Plug the card into your computer. The HOME drive mounts normally everywhere, including Windows.

    • roms/<system>/ for games, folder names match the stock card
    • .config/retroarch/system/ for BIOS files
    • read/ for epubs, which show up under Options as Reader
    • roms/bgmusic/ for menu music

    No BIOS files or games are included, and none ever will be. Use your own.

    Box art, automatically

    With the card still plugged in, this adds cover art to every game that has none, from the libretro thumbnail library. Art you already have is left alone.

    Help

    Something is wrong

    Black screen, but there is sound or the power light is on

    Almost certainly the wrong display panel.

    Nothing at all, completely dead

    Check the card is in slot TF1, not TF2. If it still does nothing, put the original card back in. If the stock firmware boots, the console is fine and the image is the problem. Rebuild it and make sure your backup came from this exact console.

    It boots to a text console or a plain launcher

    EmulationStation failed to start and fell back. Plug the card into a computer and read .config/fakers36s/ on the HOME drive, which holds the logs.

    The installer says my board is unknown

    Your card has a hardware config this project has not catalogued. Installing anyway could produce a console that does not boot, so the installer stops. Please open an issue with the fingerprint it showed you, and it can be added.

    Games run, but one system is slow

    Open Options, System Dashboard and turn on Performance mode for the 1.2 GHz clock. The battery saver caps 8 and 16 bit systems at 816 MHz on purpose, where they still run at full speed.

    Help

    Panel 1 and panel 2

    These clone boards shipped with two different screens. They are not compatible: a kernel built for one leaves the other dark. Everything else about the console is identical.

    The published image is built for panel 2, the screen on the board this project is tested on. Most A23 boards seen so far use it.

    How to tell

    There is no label to read, so you find out by booting. If after flashing you get a black or scrambled screen while the console is otherwise alive, the boot chime plays, or it responds to the power button, you have panel 1 and the wrong kernel.

    The fix

    Only the kernel differs. Your card, the system and your games all stay exactly as they are, so you swap one piece rather than starting over. You need Docker and about ten minutes.

    git clone https://github.com/Eeman1113/FakeR36S-OS
cd FakeR36S-OS/kernel && ./build.sh          # about 6 minutes

    Then write the panel 1 kernel over the one on the card. It lives at a fixed offset, outside both partitions:

    sudo dd if=out/android_boot_a23_panel1.img of=/dev/sdb \
        bs=512 seek=172032 conv=notrunc status=progress
sync

    That is the whole fix. Put the card back and power on.

    Panel 1 has not been tested on real hardware. It is built from the same device trees and should work, but nobody has confirmed it. If you try it, please say so in an issue, with or without success. That is how it stops being untested.

    Update

    Update without reflashing

    Everything of yours stays: games, saves and save states, RetroArch and per core settings, button remaps, themes, box art, BIOS and ports.

    1. Get the package

    Or download fakers36s-update-a23_panel2.tar from the release page.

    2. Put it on the card

    Plug the console's card into this computer, then pick its HOME drive.

    3. Boot the console

    Eject the card, put it back in the console and power on. The logo and spinner stay up for a few minutes while it installs, then it restarts into the new version. The log is in HOME/.config/fakers36s/update.log.

    Without Chrome or Edge

    Make a folder called update in the root of the HOME drive and copy the .tar into it, unchanged. Eject and power on. On macOS or Linux you can also run tools/update-os.sh from the repo to write the new system straight to the card.
